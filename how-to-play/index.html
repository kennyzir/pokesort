RULES & STRATEGY

How to play PokeSort 4×4

Divide 16 Pokémon into four groups of four. Each intended group shares one precise Pokémon connection.

Play while you read →

The rules

  1. Select four Pokémon you believe belong together.
  2. Submit the group. A correct set locks above the board.
  3. “One away” means exactly three selected Pokémon belong to one intended category.
  4. Solve all four groups before making four mistakes.

What counts as a connection?

Current connections use default-form type, exact dual type, introduction generation, Pokédex color, evolution-chain topology, and the Baby, Legendary, or Mythical species flags. A category must explain all four members under one declared rule in the versioned data model.

Strategy that works

Start with the narrowest fact. An exact dual type may be more useful than one shared type. Generation, Pokédex color, evolution stage, and species flags can separate a quartet when typing overlaps.

Expect overlaps. A Pokémon may fit several descriptions. Do not submit until the remaining 12 can still form plausible groups.

Use One Away carefully. Keep three likely members and replace the weakest assumption rather than rebuilding the selection at random.

Daily versus Infinite

Daily gives every player the same date-seeded board and records a local streak. Infinite produces repeatable practice boards and does not affect that streak. Archive lets you replay earlier dated boards.

Pokelike’s Daily Pokésort is a different six-Pokémon ordering puzzle. If that is the game you mean, use the Pokelike Pokésort rules and worksheet instead of these 4×4 instructions.

A verified example

A current board can include Vaporeon, Jolteon, Flareon, and Espeon as Eeveelutions. The useful fact is not their types—they differ—but that all four evolve from Eevee. Compare that narrow fact with the remaining Pokémon before submitting. The category reference lists every category family currently shipped by this site, and the Archive provides playable dated examples.
